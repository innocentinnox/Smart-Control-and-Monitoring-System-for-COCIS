Mujuni (35 s). Our system is built on the ESP32-S3 with ESP-IDF and FreeRTOS, and it has three terminals.

First, the main entrance hub. It uses two RFID readers and servo door locks to verify users at entry and exit, track who is inside, and enforce opening hours, anti-passback and an emergency break-glass override.

Second and third, lecture rooms 1 and 2. They use motion and light sensors to switch and dim their lamps automatically, use daylight, and report the energy saved to the hub.

Mujuni (30 s). My contribution. My personal role was system integration, resource synchronisation, NVS persistence and SD card storage.

First, in main.c, I designed the boot sequence along the bottom: time, storage and shared state start in the right order before the FreeRTOS tasks of the three terminals.

The hub is the single authority: the readers, the button and the rooms only send it messages.

Every shared thing has one owner

  • Who is inside, and every access decision
    Only the hub task writes it. Scans wait in its inbox and are decided one at a time.
  • The SD card
    Only the storage task touches it. Every other task queues its log lines.
  • Users, schedule, login state
    One mutex each, held for a quick copy. Readers get a copy, never a pointer.
  • Settings and the ledger in flash
    Saved to NVS with an atomic commit, so the list of people inside survives a power cut.
  • Messages on the bus
    A sequence number on every message. The receiver drops repeats and replays.
bool bus_send(src, dst, msg, wait) { xSemaphoreTake(s_send_lock[src], ...); msg->src = src; msg->seq = s_next_seq[src] + 1; bool sent = xQueueSend(s_inbox[dst], msg, wait); if (sent) s_next_seq[src] = msg->seq; xSemaphoreGive(s_send_lock[src]); return sent; }

bus.c, simplified. The per-sender lock makes "take the next number, queue the message" one step. We found that race in code review and fixed it.

Mujuni (25 s). Second, in bus.c, I built the message bus on FreeRTOS queues. Each message carries its sender and a sequence number, and a per-sender mutex keeps the numbers in order, so the receiver can safely drop duplicates and replays.

That is our answer to requirement 2.7: every shared resource has exactly one owner or one mutex.

Third, in occupancy.c and nvs_store.c, the list of people inside is protected by a mutex and saved to flash, so it survives power cuts.

Martha (40 s). The rules are one pure C function in access_policy.c, read top to bottom; the first rule that matches wins.

Unknown or disabled cards are refused. Anti-passback: you cannot enter twice without leaving, so one card cannot let two people in, and the list of who is inside stays true. Lockdown lets only security and admin through. Inside opening hours everyone passes. After hours only privileged roles pass, or ordinary users leaving during an escort window.

The lights follow the people in the room

Ochen (45 s). I am Ochen Felix, registration number 25/U/26621. I built the lecture-room lighting.

Each room has its own task that runs five times a second and decides its own lamp, so it keeps working even if the hub goes silent.

Motion makes the room occupied and the lamp fades up. A PIR only sees movement, so we wait: with no motion for 30 seconds the room counts as empty. For the last 10 seconds the lamp dims to 30 percent as a warning. [press] If someone is still there, they move, and it goes straight back to 100 percent.

Daylight harvesting, and the energy it saves

if (lux >= cfg->bright_lux) r->daylight = true; else if (lux <= cfg->dark_lux) r->daylight = false; /* in between: keep the last decision */

saved = baseline − used

Baseline: a 360 W lamp bank left on for all opening hours. Used: the real lamp level over time.

Ochen (45 s). Daylight harvesting: at 400 lux or more, the lamp stays off even in an occupied room. It only comes back when the light drops to 150 lux.

The gap between the two thresholds is hysteresis, so a passing cloud does not make the lamp flicker. That is these three lines of code.

For energy, every step adds what the lamp really used. During opening hours it also adds a baseline: a 360-watt lamp bank left on all day. The difference is the energy saved, logged every 10 minutes for each room.
